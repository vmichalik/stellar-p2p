Start with the reason

“I already have a bank account. If I need USDC in my wallet, I don't want another exchange account or another company holding a copy of my passport.”

Pactlane explores a direct trade between two people. One seller offers a fixed amount of USDC. A buyer sends the agreed euros through Revolut and receives the tokens after a payment policy authorizes release.

The product idea is to verify the few payment facts needed for that trade, without asking users to give Pactlane another full identity document. The design does not hide the payment from Revolut or the counterparty, and Stellar transactions remain public.

Keep the evidence stages separate. The public app uses synthetic data. A local compiled-WASM rehearsal joined synthetic evidence, policy code, a canonical signature, and the Soroban contract. A separate disposable testnet rehearsal passed on 25 October 2026: the reviewed WASM released synthetic PLTEST, rejected replay, and mutually resolved another order with a half split. The auth-entry serialization bug found on an earlier recovery attempt was fixed before the passing run. No bank proof or real USDC was used. Read the captured testnet evidence.

The offer example is synthetic: €20 is fiat_minor = 2000 (cents), and 22 seven-decimal tokens is token_amount = 220000000 atoms. The testnet rehearsal used PLTEST, not USDC. This is not a bank value, a quote, or evidence of a fiat payment.

1. The system has two kinds of evidence

The proof service attests to selected bytes from an authenticated TLS session. The off-chain policy interprets those bytes against a confirmed escrow order and signs a claim. The Soroban contract checks that claim against its own stored terms. The chain does not verify the bank response itself.

The policy receives trusted evidence and a fresh chain view, recomputes the recipient commitment from private offer data, reserves the payment identity, then signs. A browser progress message alone cannot authorize release.

2. The contract is a small state machine

A seller funds one whole offer. A buyer may reserve it once, then explicitly lock it before sending fiat. A valid policy claim releases the tokens. Before payment is locked, an offer can be refunded under specific rules. After lock, there is no automatic timeout back to the seller.

Method Who authorizes? What it enforces
fund Seller Positive fixed amounts, EUR, Revolut, recipient commitment, offer lifetime at most seven days, reservation at most one hour; transfers tokens into escrow.
reserve Buyer Offer remains open; buyer differs from seller and contract. Starts the reservation window.
begin_payment Reserved buyer Must happen before pay_by. Writes locked_at and moves to Paying.
release Permissionless relay; signed by policy key Matches exact order, verifies time bounds, signature, expiry, and unused nullifier; pays the claim's buyer once.
refund Seller when Funded; anyone after unused reservation expires Only returns escrow before the payment lock.
resolve Seller and buyer Authorizes a specific split from Reserved or Paying. It does not inspect payment proof.

Order records and used-nullifier markers use persistent storage with TTL extension. The global anti-replay rule also depends on a durable off-chain registry spanning deployments. The chain only sees a 32-byte recipient commitment, not the bank recipient or salt.

3. Policy turns evidence into a narrow authorization

The policy is the central trust boundary. It reads a verified record, current order and chain time, private offer data, and replay storage. It checks that one specific payment fits one specific locked order. Only then does it ask the operator-controlled signer to approve the canonical claim.

Two hashes solve different problems

Recipient commitment: a salted hash of the exact provider-issued recipient ID in a fixed source namespace. It lets the chain bind an offer to its intended recipient without storing the identifier. The policy recomputes it from private offer data and verified evidence.

Payment nullifier: a deterministic hash of fixed namespace, source origin, payer scope, and stable payment ID. It deliberately excludes order, network, amount, recipient, and timestamp so changing those does not make a transfer usable again. It is a stable pseudonym, not anonymity.

Claim digest: SHA-256 over canonical ScVal-XDR that includes the network ID, escrow address, and every claim field. Ed25519 signs the raw 32-byte digest. The contract recomputes it and checks its own copy of the order terms.

The live normalizer still fails closed because real bank fields, units, and stable source/recipient identities have not been accepted and mapped. Synthetic fixture fields are not Revolut's claimed schema.

4. TLSNotary authenticates the transcript, not the judgment

TLSNotary uses an MPC-TLS prover/verifier flow to establish that selected response bytes came from a TLS session with a pinned server. The verifier's cryptographic result does not by itself determine which account paid, what “completed” means, which amount unit applies, or whether the payment matches this order.

The verifier source patch removes transcript formatting from INFO and DEBUG logs and rejects Proxy commitment mode before opening a proxy-target connection, while preserving MPC mode. The patched source passed cargo check --offline. It was not release-built, deployed, or runtime-retested; the log behavior of a running service remains unverified.

Current TLSNotary diagnostics have exercised setup and public responses. Those are not authenticated payment proofs. The policy's live normalizer still rejects all live records.

5. Privacy means minimizing extra data

Pactlane cannot make the exchange anonymous. It aims to reduce the amount of additional identity material a user gives to a new intermediary for one trade.

Visible to What may be visible Boundary
Bank Customer identity and transfer activity Pactlane cannot hide those facts from the bank.
Counterparty Payment instructions and payment recipient/sender details A direct payment necessarily exposes relevant payment details.
Proof service Selected TLS transcript data needed for proof The source patch addresses formatting at a specific verifier function; deployed log behavior is unverified.
Policy host Evidence fields, private offer information, stable pseudonymous IDs Only the operator and authorized readers should access this data.
Stellar ledger Addresses, order state, amount, timing, token movement, hashes Chain activity is public.

Never put bank identifiers, recipient salts, credentials, raw payment responses, or private keys in public UI payloads or source control.

6. The test workbench has a real host boundary

There is now source for a Node host and same-origin browser workbench. The host serves a restricted static client, protects API requests with a bearer capability, checks the configured origin and host, limits request size, and exposes wallet requests without holding wallet keys. The browser keeps the capability in memory after reading the link fragment and sends it in an authorization header.

Recovery uses two different signatures: the seller signs the Soroban authorization entry for the exact contract call; the buyer signs the final transaction envelope as its source account. The adapter validates the entries and signatures and refuses extra invocations. The local test fixtures cover supported simulation shapes.

Testnet scope: the recorded run passed release, replay rejection, and a mutual half split using PLTEST, the reviewed WASM, and programmatic disposable keys. It did not test Freighter UI or authenticated bank evidence. The deployed testnet asset is not USDC.

The host can run without a proof adapter or signer; in that mode the proof gate stays closed. Real authenticated verifier evidence, a live indexer/policy configuration, and an accepted payment normalizer are not yet established. The bearer capability is access control for this workbench, not proof of a user's legal identity.

7. Operations, failures, and what the checks mean

Submission is disabled by default. Before submission, the host reserves an action in a durable journal. It stores the transaction hash before sending. A timeout does not mean failure: the host reconciles the same hash rather than creating a second action. A receipt is only complete after the confirmed envelope, order state, and token events match.

8. Explain it without overclaiming

Thirty seconds

“Pactlane is a prototype for direct EUR-to-USDC trades between two people. The seller locks one fixed token offer in a Soroban escrow. The buyer reserves it and confirms the payment window before sending euros. A policy service checks evidence against that exact order and signs a release claim; the contract checks its own terms before paying once. The public app is synthetic. A local compiled-contract rehearsal passed, and a disposable testnet rehearsal passed release, replay rejection, and mutual recovery with synthetic PLTEST. We have not validated an authenticated bank payment or real USDC settlement.”

Two minutes

“Some people already have a bank account and want USDC on Stellar, but would rather not open another exchange account and hand over another identity document. Pactlane explores a direct trade: the seller puts up the tokens first, and the buyer pays the agreed euros directly.

The buyer confirms an on-chain payment lock before paying. The intended proof flow uses TLSNotary to authenticate selected bank-response bytes. An off-chain policy checks their meaning against the order, recipient commitment, payment time, and replay registry, then signs a canonical claim. The contract checks that signature and its own stored terms. It does not verify the TLSNotary proof, so users still trust the policy operator's interpretation and signer key.

The public app is synthetic, and the completed local compiled-WASM rehearsal used synthetic evidence. A disposable testnet rehearsal with the reviewed WASM passed a synthetic token release, replay rejection, and mutual half-split recovery. It used PLTEST and programmatic disposable wallet keys, not Freighter UI. We have not validated a real authenticated payment or real USDC settlement.”

Questions people may ask

Is it trustless?

No. The policy operator is trusted to interpret the payment evidence correctly and protect the signing key. The contract independently checks escrow rules and signatures.

Is it private or anonymous?

No. The bank sees its account activity, the counterparty sees payment details, and Stellar reveals addresses and token movements. The goal is to minimize extra identity-document collection and disclose selected evidence to the policy service.

Is it fully zero knowledge?

No. TLSNotary uses MPC-TLS and selective disclosure. The contract receives a signed claim; it does not verify a zero-knowledge proof or TLS transcript.

What happens if proof fails after the buyer pays?

The escrow remains in Paying. It does not time out to the seller. The buyer can retry with valid evidence or both parties can authorize a split. If they cannot agree, tokens may remain held.

Can one payment be used twice?

The nullifier is derived from stable payer scope and payment ID. The contract tracks use on one deployment; the policy registry and complete chain reconciliation are needed across deployments. Real-source identity mapping is not yet validated.

What is actually live?

The captured disposable testnet rehearsal passed release, replay rejection, and mutual recovery with the reviewed WASM and synthetic PLTEST. It did not use authenticated bank evidence, USDC, or Freighter UI. The release gate remains closed.
